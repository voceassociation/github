How VOCE works: evidence, independence and institutional discipline.

VOCE’s credibility depends less on the breadth of its subjects than on the consistency of the method applied to them.

01

Source hierarchy

VOCE gives priority to primary or near-primary sources: peer-reviewed research, official datasets, technical documentation, regulations, institutional reports, trial registries, infrastructure specifications and first-party disclosures. Secondary reporting is useful for discovery and context, but it should not silently replace the underlying source.

For contemporary subjects, recency matters. A historical study can remain important, but VOCE avoids presenting older evidence as if it described the current technological, clinical or institutional environment.

02

Evidence and interpretation

Every VOCE text should make it possible to distinguish documented facts, methodological interpretation and editorial analysis. Correlation is not presented as causality. Small samples, proxy measures, laboratory settings, geographic limits and conflicts of interest matter.

The purpose is not to make every article timid. It is to make strong claims only where the evidentiary structure can support them.

03

Scientific committee and expert review

The scientific committee and expert network provide disciplinary challenge rather than decorative prestige. Their role is to test mechanisms, expose blind spots, question terminology and help VOCE avoid ideological, emotional or commercial drift.

VOCE’s international vantage points also act as a methodological check: an assumption that appears universal in Paris may look different in Dubai, Hangzhou, Shanghai or Hong Kong.

04

Corrections, versions and traceability

Research outputs should preserve versions, citations and persistent identifiers where appropriate. Editorial work should be corrected when material errors are identified. A correction is not treated as reputational failure; it is part of research discipline.

For technical and regulatory material, the date of the claim matters because systems, models and rules change quickly.

05

Use of artificial intelligence

VOCE can use AI as a research and production instrument for retrieval, comparison, drafting, translation, coding or workflow support. AI output is not treated as a source. Claims that matter must be grounded in identifiable evidence, and human responsibility remains with the person or institution publishing the work.

For AI-governance research in particular, VOCE applies to itself the distinction it makes elsewhere: a model can assist cognition, but authority and accountability must remain explicit.

06

Independence, conflicts and funding

VOCE’s editorial and research independence should remain distinguishable from commercial, institutional or philanthropic relationships. Where a relationship materially affects a publication or project, it should be disclosed.

The standard is not the absence of partners. It is the ability to preserve method, attribution and the right to publish conclusions that are not dictated by a partner’s interests.

07

Scope and limits

VOCE is an educational, research and cultural institution. It does not provide medical diagnosis or treatment, and its publications do not replace professional clinical, legal, financial or regulatory advice.

This boundary allows VOCE to work seriously on health, psychiatry, governance and institutions without confusing public knowledge with professional care.
