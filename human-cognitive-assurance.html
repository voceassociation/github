The human in the loop is a cognitive system, not a checkbox.

VOCE Human Cognitive Assurance adds the human-factors layer to institutional agent governance. Twenty-four controls examine what automation does to attention, verification, memory, decision quality, fatigue, skills and social signals. The VOCE Cognitive Agent grounds analysis in the VOCE corpus and returns the controls that require examination.

Describe an agentic workflow, interface or human oversight problem. The agent searches the VOCE corpus and maps the relevant cognitive controls. It analyses systems and workflows only.

Human factors

Eight human-system domains.

The framework evaluates the operating environment rather than diagnosing individuals. Each control asks whether the institution has evidence that human attention, judgment and intervention remain viable.

Control catalogue

Twenty-four permanent controls.

Each control has a stable identifier, expected evidence and links back to the VOCE corpus. The catalogue is static HTML so search engines, auditors and institutional readers can inspect it without running the agent.

Controls are operational assurance questions, not clinical screening criteria.

VOCE · Human Cognitive Assurance

Clinical boundary.

VOCE Human Cognitive Assurance evaluates systems, interfaces, workflows and organisational conditions. It does not diagnose, screen, treat or profile an individual's mental health, cognitive disorder or neuropsychological condition. Any clinical interpretation remains with a qualified psychologist, neuropsychologist, psychiatrist or other authorised professional.
