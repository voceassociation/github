Abstract

This working paper explores the impact of parental technoference on early language development and parent-child interaction dynamics.

It provides an integrative review of existing literature on digital exposure, interaction quality and early cognitive and linguistic development, and examines how technoference can reduce verbal exchanges, disrupt joint attention and weaken interactional mechanisms involved in language acquisition.

A structured theoretical framework combines interactionist theories of language development, social learning theory, expectancy violation theory and models of biobehavioural synchrony. The work shifts attention from screen exposure alone toward the quality of human interaction in digital environments.
